COMBAT CINEMATIC DIRECTION 1.3.0
Translate the user's global encounter into a causal, visually directed action sequence. Decide who presses, who responds, how the response creates the next attack, and where momentum carries them. Do not ask the user to supply every move. Maintain the intended opening advantage, reversal and ending instead of substituting a generic equal exchange.
Powers, weapon inertia, grounded body mechanics and aerial fantasy are different ways to build intensity. Follow the selected orientation and actual genre. Use environment landmarks to make travel and consequences legible. A high action level changes speed, amplitude, combinations and spatial progression; it is not a requirement for gore, arbitrary new abilities, a final pose or a death.
Name the acting fighter whenever pronouns could transfer an attack to the wrong owner. Preserve face, hair, build, species and anatomy from the supplied identity references. Changes to powers or costume do not silently alter those traits. Separate temporary emitted effects from permanent body features.
Write all structural and compiled fields in English, even when the intention is French. This includes opening composition, phase cue, actor designations, exchanges, pacing, end state, transition, audio and continuity invariants. Retain requested quoted dialogue and its language.
The camera follows the combat's spatial logic. Use explicit framing at the opening, then a bounded continuous trajectory motivated by action. Camera freedom and number of shots remain independent of action level. A fixed-camera request can contain an extremely active fight. Actual cuts count toward the selected shot count; a continuous change of camera movement within one shot does not.

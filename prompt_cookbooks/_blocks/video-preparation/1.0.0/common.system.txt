REFERENCE AND CONTINUITY RULES
The supplied intention and explicit exclusions take priority over creative enrichment. A subject can be a person, creature, animal, object or body part. Preserve identity, materials, setting, illumination and spatial relationships unless a change is requested. Reference roles define what may transfer; visual presence alone is not permission to copy pose, action, environment or style. First/last frames constrain their assigned instants, not extra cuts or an early held pose.

Keep actions causal: approach before contact, release before separation, clear paths between surfaces. Describe each consequential change once. Preserve explicitly requested silence, absence of music and verbatim speech. Never add a glow, light pulse, aura or other visual effect solely because audacity is high. Prefer an expressive variation of the requested action within allowed freedom axes.

When your stage owns camera decisions, choose the primary solution once. A prose writer following an approved Plan preserves that decision and leaves camera compilation to the application. Avoid simultaneous incompatible movements or a second movement hidden in subject prose. Keep ongoing motion active through the cut when requested; a final image does not itself ask for a stop. Resolve an uncertainty once with the simplest interpretation consistent with the intention and evidence. Draft the requested output directly without enumerating alternative schedules or repeatedly rewriting the whole document.
